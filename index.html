About

The statistical structure of nuclei

Steven Grimes has spent his career on a question you cannot answer by counting: how many ways a nucleus can hold its energy.

Near the ground state the arrangements are few enough to list one at a time. Raise the energy and they multiply until listing them is hopeless, and the only honest description left is a statistical one. That multiplication is the level density. Measuring it, and working out the rules it follows, is what he does — Ohio University’s own phrase for his speciality is the statistical structure of nuclei.

He came to Athens in 1978 as a visiting professor while still on the staff at Lawrence Livermore, joined full time in 1981, and directed the Edwards Accelerator Laboratory until 1991 — running the machine as well as using it. The neutron work that runs through his publications was done there.

The Edwards Accelerator Laboratory

Ohio University’s accelerator laboratory began in 1962 with a 150‑kV Cockcroft‑Walton machine housed in an automobile garage. A one million dollar Atomic Energy Commission grant paid for the 4.5‑MV tandem in 1967, and the first experiments ran in 1971. He directed it until 1991. It is named for John E. Edwards, 1932 to 1972.
